# MyInvois, SST and PDPA — schema impact on 007 / 010 / 005

Research for the finance and consent schema, checked against `docs/bd/proposal-content-pack-v2.md` §1.5, §3.2, §7.8, Appendix C and `packages/contract/src/domain/hrdc-finance.ts`. Sources cited inline; each accessed 13 Sep 2026 unless noted.

## 1. MyInvois e-invoicing

The IRBM e-Invoice Guideline v4.6 (published 7 Dec 2025) sets the phase table: >RM100m — 1 Aug 2024; RM25–100m — 1 Jan 2025; RM5–25m — 1 Jul 2025; **up to RM5m — 1 Jan 2026** (Table 1.1). Taxpayers with turnover under RM1,000,000 are exempted entirely from issuing e-invoices, including self-billed ones (§1.6.1(e)) — [IRBM e-Invoice Guideline v4.6 PDF](https://www.hasil.gov.my/media/fzagbaj2/irbm-e-invoice-guideline.pdf). So a provider at RM1–5m turnover is squarely in the mandatory Phase 4 band, live now, not a future date — §1.5's "mandatory above RM1m" is close but should say "and up to RM5m by 1 Jan 2026."

LHDN extended Phase 4's penalty-free relaxation (issuing consolidated e-invoices without Section 120 enforcement) to 31 Dec 2027, with full enforcement from 1 Jan 2028, per e-Invoice Specific Guideline v4.7 (20 Apr 2026) — [VATupdate summary of v4.7](https://www.vatupdate.com/2026/04/23/malaysia-updates-e-invoicing-framework-specific-guide-v4-7-issued-and-phase-4-relaxation-extended-to-31-december-2027/) (v4.7 PDF itself could not be parsed this session; treat the date as needing a direct re-check against hasil.gov.my before it goes in a contract).

Mandatory fields confirmed in v4.6's field list (pp. 43–47): Buyer's TIN, Buyer's registration/MyKad/passport number, Buyer's SST registration number (if SST-registered), Supplier's 5-digit MSIC code, per-line Classification code, plus the IRBM Unique Identifier Number, validation link and QR code assigned only on validation, and the digital signature. Rejection/cancellation: either party may reject or cancel **within 72 hours of validation**; after that, only a new document (credit/debit/refund note) can adjust it (§2.3.5/§2.3.9, verbatim "If the e-Invoice is not rejected or cancelled within 72 hours, no cancellation would be allowed"). Self-billed type codes cover income the payer must document on the payee's behalf; not required for employment income, pension, alimony, certain dividend distributions, zakat, and listed securities/derivatives trades (§1.6.7). For TrainOS this matches trainer/associate day-rate payables (007's `rate_card_trainer_days`) as self-billed invoices.

## 2. SST on training services — the significant finding

Training and coaching is its own taxable category under **Group G (Professionals)** of the Service Tax Regulations 2018 — RMCD's *Guide on Consultancy, Training or Coaching Services* — taxed at the current **8% rate (since 1 March 2024)**, registration threshold **RM500,000** in 12 months. The SST exemption for "education services" applies only to institutions **registered under the Education Act 1996** (schools, colleges, universities) — a private, HRD Corp-accredited corporate training provider is not that, and is taxable regardless of any educational component [MySST — Guide on Consultancy, Training or Coaching Services](https://mysst.customs.gov.my/assets/document/Industry%20Guides/GI/Guide%20on%20Consultancy_Training_or_Coaching%20Services%2020211011.pdf); rate confirmed via [BDO Malaysia SST rate/scope update](https://www.bdo.my/getmedia/e257a151-1a41-4554-ac72-c4071c9a2d94/BDO-SST-Expansion-(FA).pdf).

This directly contradicts `010`'s default: `invoices.sst_reason DEFAULT 'TRAINING_EXEMPT'` with `sst_rate DEFAULT 0`, and the enum literally spells the assumption `'TRAINING_EXEMPT'`. Unless [CLIENT] has a specific carve-out (e.g. a formal-education partnership), the correct default posture for a corporate training provider is **standard-rated at 8%**, with exemption as the reasoned exception, not the default. I found no HRD Corp-levy-funded exemption from SST — the levy funds the buyer's payment, it doesn't change the supplier's taxable-service status.

## 3. PDPA (amended) — already in force, not pending

- **Retention (s.10, unchanged principle):** a controller may not keep personal data longer than necessary for the purpose collected, and must destroy/permanently delete it once no longer required — [pdp.gov.my — Privacy](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/en/privacy/).
- **DPO (new s.12A)** and **breach notification (new s.12B)**: both in force **1 June 2025**. Breach: notify the Commissioner within **72 hours** of becoming aware; notify affected individuals "without unnecessary delay," no later than **7 days** after the Commissioner notification, if significant harm is likely. Non-compliance with notification duties: up to RM250,000 and/or 2 years' imprisonment — [Personal Data Protection Guideline on Data Breach Notification (25 Feb 2025), via DLA Piper Privacy Matters summary](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2025/03/malaysia-guidelines-issued-on-data-breach-notification-and-data-protection-officer-appointment/).
- **Cross-border transfer (amended s.129), in force 1 Apr 2025**: the Minister-approved whitelist is gone; a controller may transfer abroad only after itself assessing the destination has "substantially similar" or "adequate" protection, or under a specified exception — the self-assessment burden is now on TrainOS/[CLIENT] as controller, not a government list — [Mayer Brown — Key Amendments to Malaysia's PDPA](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/07/from-legislative-reform-to-practical-guidance-key-amendments-to-malaysias-pdpa-and-the-launch-of-cross-border-transfer-guidelines).
- **Penalty uplift:** several offences' maximum penalty rose roughly fivefold, e.g. RM300,000/2yrs to **RM1,000,000/3yrs** for the most serious breaches of the data protection principles — [Lexology — Overview of Amendments](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=69c66cb8-6720-4758-aeba-72a0ca22729d); §1.5's "fines up to RM1m" figure is right but reads as prospective when it has been live since June/April 2025.
- **New s.43A** data portability right, subject to technical feasibility.

## 4. HRD Corp claim documentation retention

Multiple secondary trade sources converge on **at least 7 years** for attendance sheets, evaluation forms, invoices and proof of payment held for audit, and invoices must show employer name, programme title, training date and SST number. I could not locate the specific hrdcorp.gov.my Training Provider Circular text confirming the 7-year figure directly this session (the circulars index page loaded, but no circular text was retrievable) — treat 7 years as the working assumption and re-confirm against a named circular (likely in the 3/2021 or later series) before it is written into a retention policy or a claim-audit SLA.

## Proposal-pack sentences to correct

- §1.5: *"e-Invoicing mandatory above RM 1m turnover via MyInvois"* → state Phase 4 (up to RM5m) went live 1 Jan 2026, with a penalty-free relaxation to 31 Dec 2027 and full enforcement from 1 Jan 2028 (re-verify the 2027/2028 dates against hasil.gov.my directly before sending).
- §1.5: *"PDPA (amended): mandatory DPO, breach notification, new cross-border regime, fines up to RM1m"* → these are already in force (DPO/breach notification since 1 Jun 2025, cross-border since 1 Apr 2025), not upcoming obligations — reads better as a present-tense constraint.
- §1.5/Appendix: add a bullet that training/coaching services are themselves SST-taxable (Group G, 8%, RM500k threshold) unless [CLIENT] holds a specific exemption — this is currently absent from the operating-constraints list entirely.
- §7.8: *"prices exclude SST"* is fine commercially, but add a footnote that [CLIENT]'s own training invoices to its clients are very likely SST-taxable output tax, separate from TrainOS's fees — several training providers wrongly assume education-style exemption.
- §3.4: the cross-border DPA clause should reference the post-1-Apr-2025 adequacy self-assessment (s.129) rather than imply a government-approved country list, which no longer exists.
