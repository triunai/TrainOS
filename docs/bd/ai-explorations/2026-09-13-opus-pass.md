## 1 · Claimable-date projection

**Decision.** A projection, not a stored fact, and not "grant approval + 14 days". The compliance
refresh splits it: in-house `start ≥ approval + 14d`; public `+3d` until 31 Dec 2026, then `+14d`
from 1 Jan 2027 (HRD-015 → HRD-022). So the answer depends on `delivery_mode` **and** which side of
1 Jan 2027 the training date falls — the bitemporal case 009's T3 pins. Add the term the pack missed:
one query round, **5 calendar days** to reply or the application expires. It returns a clean-path
date *and* a contingency date, never a bare one. Its only free parameter — days from agreement to
submission — is tenant config, never "submit today".

Ship it as a read, not `/follow-ups/{id}/draft`: `FollowUp` presumes a proposal (005 defers its
`proposal_id` FK to 007). `ENQUIRY_REPLY_DRAFT` joins `GOVERNED_ACTION_TYPES` only, the
`PROPOSAL_DRAFT` pattern, read from a worker row and never generated in a request (doc 08 §8).

**Change.** `ClaimableDateProjection { assumedSubmission, earliestStart, contingentStart,
commencementDeadline, queryDeadline, ruleIds[], rulesAsOf, basis }` in
`packages/contract/src/domain/enquiries.ts`; `ENQUIRY_REPLY_DRAFT` at `actions.ts:102`; RPC
`core.project_claimable_date` over `core.resolve_rules()` (009:391); zero-token tool in
`packages/agent-runtime/src/tools/definitions.ts`; metric cell on
`apps/web/src/features/enquiries/EnquiryDetailPage.tsx`.
**Pack.** 017 after 016.
**Eval.** 60 enquiries with real grant/start dates, exact match 100%; one case straddles 1 Jan 2027
and returns 3 days for a December start and 14 for a January start *in the same run*.
**Risk.** A date quoted without the contingency is wrong by weeks when a query lands — and it went
out with confidence. Render the assumed submission and the citation with it.

## 2 · Jury vendor independence — and a PDPA defect underneath it

**Decision.** Rebind `STRONG_2` to Gemini 3.1 Pro and `STRONG_3` to GPT-5.6 Terra, per pack §4.1.
STRONG_1 and STRONG_2 are both Anthropic today, so a 2-of-3 `ESCALATE_JURY` verdict on a
first-of-kind send can be two Anthropic votes. The rebind is free: $2/$12 against Opus 5's $5/$25.

The larger finding is a tier down. `CHEAP` runs `OPPORTUNITY_CONVERT` and `ENQUIRY_ARCHIVE` — raw
enquiry bodies carrying names, emails, phones — on `deepseek-chat`, DeepSeek's own API, which the
storage research says stores on PRC servers and is **not usable for PII**. A compliance defect, not a
price mismatch: rebind to Qwen3.7 Flash via OpenRouter with a non-China host pin. Keep Haiku 4.5 on
FAST/FAST-UI (deliberate; delta under 10%) and rewrite pack §4.1 to match.

**Change.** `routing/config.ts` `DEFAULT_TIERS` (CHEAP, FAST_UI, STRONG_2, STRONG_3, DEEP_THINK);
`allowedProviders`/`piiClass` on `TierBinding`; assert three distinct providers per jury run or
record `juryDegraded`; `ModelTier`/`RoutingEntry` in `packages/contract/src/domain/ai-ops.ts`.
**Pack.** Amend 013 — it owns the tier and budget tables, authored and not applied.
**Eval.** A jury whose three tiers resolve to two providers fails the promotion gate; a routing test
asserts no PII-class action reaches a PRC-hosted binding.
**Risk.** A third vendor is a third DPA and a third cross-border self-assessment under amended s.129,
where the whitelist is gone and the controller carries it.

**Pack.** None; runtime and contract only.
**Eval.** `GOVERNED_ACTION_TYPES` ⊆ routing entries; the TNA fixture run records tier and jury mode
in its trace, not an implicit default.
**Risk.** A silent default makes an un-juried action look governed.

## 4 · Utilisation, defined once

**Decision.** HRD Corp publishes **no** definition; the research found only the trigger condition.
Define it in the contract as an explicitly inferred quantity, and say so on screen:
`utilisation = claims approved in the calendar year ÷ levy contributed that same year`, window
1 Jan–31 Dec, denominator the year's contribution, **not** the accumulated balance.

Second, and this changes what ships: every value carries `coverage`. TrainOS sees only its own
`hrdc_packets.claim_value_sen`, and brokers send each employer to three to five providers at once, so
TrainOS-only history reads systematically low — inverting the pitch. **Gate the 15% flag on
`coverage <> 'TRAINOS_ONLY'`**; it needs a statement or a declared figure. The zero-inference rules
ship now: balance > RM 50,000 and the 24-month forfeiture clock above RM 10,000 (Circular 7/2019).
`core.hrdc_levy_statements`, zero consumers today, is the series both read.

**Change.** `LevyPosition { asOf, balance, contributedYtd, claimsApprovedYtd, utilisation, coverage,
basis, projectedYearEnd, forfeitureAt }` in `domain/organisations.ts`;
`core.hrdc_levy_statements` gains `contributed_ytd_sen`, `claims_approved_ytd_sen`, `coverage`,
`source_document_id`; RPC `core.levy_position`; trend and countdown in
`apps/web/src/features/relationships/CrossSellPage.tsx`.
**Pack.** 017; staleness sweep in 015.
**Eval.** RM 45,000 at 20% must not flag; a `TRAINOS_ONLY` org at apparent 15% utilisation produces
**no** 15% flag, not a flag with a caveat. Projection error ≤ 2%.
**Risk.** Telling a client they face a deduction they will not incur is the product's worst false
positive — a claim about their money, in the opening sentence of the call.

## 6 · Competency taxonomy

**Decision.** Not a table in Phase 1, nor free text: a **controlled vocabulary** —
`core.competency_terms (tenant_id NULL = global, term, synonyms[], source)` seeded from the client's
own `programmes.category` and `outcomes[]`, with `tna_gaps.competency_term_id` nullable so an
unmapped gap is *visible*, not silently string-matched. That gives the eval a denominator
without pretending to be a skills graph.

From the vector research: 009's `embedding` column is the one object never executed in its intended
form, so 018 creates it for real. The live hazard is `hnsw.iterative_scan` defaulting to `off` — with
RLS adding a `tenant_id` predicate over an HNSW scan, top-k **silently returns fewer than k rows**
for a small tenant; the retrieval RPC sets `relaxed_order`. Storage stays pgvector — Pinecone's $50/month floor
exceeds pgvector's whole marginal cost at every modelled scale — with the Small compute add-on as the
step past Micro's ~15k vectors.

**Change.** `TnaGap.competencyTermId` in `packages/contract/src/domain/proposals.ts`; 018 adds
`core.competency_terms`, the real embedding column + HNSW index, and `core.match_programmes`.
**Pack.** 018.
**Eval.** TNA-0042 keeps PRG-0031 0.91 above PRG-0018 0.78; TNA-0054 (`gaps: []`) returns no
recommendation; a tenant-isolation test proves a 2%-of-corpus tenant still gets k results.
**Risk.** `iterative_scan = off` is a silent wrong answer, not an error — it presents as "the AI
found nothing", which nobody files.

## 7 · Ingestion pipeline, and who writes a global rule

**Where it runs.** A **Node worker on Render**, not an Edge Function — doc 08 §10 rules Edge
Functions out, and the Supabase research confirms why: a separate 2s CPU budget that PDF-to-text and
hashing would blow, plus `EarlyDrop`. It claims through `app.claim_jobs` as
`service_role`; pg_cron in 015 schedules database-internal sweeps only, never `net.http_post` (beta,
~200 rps, unlogged, blind to its own outcome).

**Jury.** Rule-diff is jury-gated: `RuleChangeSet.extractedBy` becomes a jury result — three STRONG
models, 2-of-3, dissent recorded. A hallucinated rule becomes the constraint every future engagement
is checked against.

**The global write.** "Finance approves" is imprecise; 009's header settles it — a global rule
(`tenant_id IS NULL`) is `service_role` only, with no platform-admin role by design. So CMP-05's
`RULE_CHANGE_APPROVE` at `approver_role FINANCE` produces a **platform-review request**, applied by
the worker after approval; a tenant ADMIN can only create an override. `RuleChange` gains
`scope: 'GLOBAL' | 'TENANT_OVERRIDE'` so the screen says which.

**Change.** `extractedBy`/`scope` in `packages/contract/src/domain/hrdc-finance.ts`; source versions
in `domain/knowledge.ts`; new `packages/agent-runtime/src/agents/rule-watcher.ts`;
`core.knowledge_source_versions` in 018.
**Pack.** 018, scheduled in 015.
**Eval.** The circular-diff trap ("§3.2 is amended to match §2.1" against "§2.1 — see §3.2") resolves
to withheld below 0.80 every run; recall 1.00 on historical BINDING changes; a tenant ADMIN's
approval writes an override, never a global row.
**Risk.** Fetched circulars are untrusted by design — hash and diff before any model sees content;
extraction gets no tools.

## 8 · The collections number, recomputed

**Decision.** State the formula and RM 150,000 survives: cash released once = average daily revenue
× ΔDSO. At §0's RM 300,000/month that is RM 9,863/day; ΔDSO 15 gives **RM 147,945**. Exploration 6's
RM 200,000 scaled the *balance* instead of daily revenue — wrong formula. It is cash, never P&L.

Two corrections ride along. The human rung is `HUMAN_CALL` at **day 60, fourth of five**; the
brainstorm and pack §2.3 say "step 3". `REMINDER_3` at day 45 stays drafted (the 010 CHECK forbids
only `AUTONOMOUS`), so `CollectionsQueueScreen.tsx`'s "the agent STOPS" copy is wrong, not the schema
— question 9 decides whether to re-number the seed instead. And `ChannelConsent.granted` must gate
the send inside effect planning, not only the panel.

**Change.** 016 seeds `core.collection_rules` and `core.aging_buckets`; the consent gate is amended
into 011 (which owns effect planning); copy fix in `finance/CollectionsQueueScreen.tsx`.
**Pack.** 016, plus an 011 amendment.
**Eval.** 34 days → `REMINDER_2`, 80 → `TRADING_HOLD` via `core.collection_stage_for()`; a
consent-negative contact refused **in SQL**, asserted in the pin not the UI; recall ≥ 0.98 on
"should have escalated".
**Risk.** A reminder sent without consent breaches the data-protection principles, now RM1,000,000 /
3 years — and the amendment makes the processor directly liable, so the vendor carries it too.

## Ten questions for Alex, ordered by how much hinges

1. **Phase 0 access to 12–24 months of enquiries, proposals and claim outcomes — and your real
   volumes and average claimable value?** Every golden set, every §0 number, and whether items 5–8
   are Phase 1.
2. **For how many clients do you hold the levy statement, the year's contribution and claim history,
   and in what form?** Decides whether the 15% rule ships at all, or the radar ships as balance and
   forfeiture only.
3. **Of claims in the last 24 months, how many were queried or rejected, why, and at what cost?** The
   only honest size for the guard, the only source for the 3% figure, and the only read on the
   query clock.
4. **Who signs off a proposal above what value, who approves a rule change — and should anyone change
   a rule for every tenant?** The 011 policy seed and the global-versus-override path.
5. **Where do enquiries arrive, who touches them first, and must WhatsApp inbound reach the reply
   chain in Phase 1?** The contract's only worked example is `EMAIL`.
6. **How many accredited trainers, do you hold HRD-TDF and TTT expiry dates, and how often has
   availability lost an engagement?** Decides `hrd_tdf_valid_to`, and whether the 72-hour hold needs
   a release job or a read-time check.
7. **After a client agrees, how many days before you submit the grant — and should a quoted date
   assume clean approval or carry the contingency?** The projection's only free parameter.
8. **What error rate on a drafted client-facing reply is unacceptable, and what evidence moves an
   action from Suggest to Act-with-approval?** The promotion bars.
9. **Your real receivables and DSO, and where do you want a human on the phone — day 45 or 60?**
   Day 45 re-numbers the 016 seed instead of fixing screen copy.
10. **Are your training invoices SST standard-rated at 8%, or do you hold an exemption?** 010 defaults
    to `TRAINING_EXEMPT`; the research says training is Group G taxable, so that default is wrong.
